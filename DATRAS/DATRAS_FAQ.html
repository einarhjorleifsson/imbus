DATRAS Frequently Asked Questions

ICES Data Centre — DATRAS

Modified

July 1, 2026

Note on this document. This is a restructured and updated version of the ICES DATRAS FAQs (version 3.0, 2014). Field names have been updated to the current standard; legacy names are shown in parentheses. DataType P (size-stratified subsampling) has been added throughout. The survey list reflects the current DATRAS holdings rather than the 2014 snapshot. The source document is docs_external/DATRAS_documentation/DATRAS_FAQs.pdf.

Questions and observations marked [IMBUS] were added during the IMBUS project and are not part of the original ICES document.

Overview of DATRAS

What is DATRAS?

DATRAS is an online database of trawl surveys hosted by ICES. It stores data from internationally coordinated fish trawl surveys in ICES area 27 in a standardised format and provides a data quality check upon upload. The DATRAS portal offers online services for uploading and downloading survey data.

What can DATRAS data be used for?

DATRAS is used by ICES expert groups, national institutes, and researchers for verifying, storing, and obtaining fisheries-independent sampling data. The data can be used in stock assessments, biodiversity studies, and other fisheries-related projects. All data products are accessible under ICES Data Policy.

What is the timespan of DATRAS data?

Each survey has its own timeline. The oldest records are from 1965 (NS-IBTS). A full overview of survey coverage, including first and last year and valid haul counts, is given in the D2.2 surveys table.

Which surveys are available via DATRAS?

DATRAS currently holds data from 29 surveys covering the Northeast Atlantic (as of 2026). The 2014 version of this document listed 15 surveys; the inventory has grown substantially. See the D2.2 surveys table for the full current list. Surveys include NS-IBTS, BITS, BTS, EVHOE, IE-IGFS, NIGFS, FR-CGFS, SP-NORTH, SP-PORC, SP-ARSA, PT-IBTS, and many others.

Which data products can I get from DATRAS?

All uploaded survey data can be downloaded as raw exchange data (HH, HL, CA record types). Some surveys also offer standard derived data products computed by the ICES expert groups:

Product Description
CPUEL CPUE-by-length per haul
CPUEA CPUE-by-age per haul
IDX Survey abundance indices
CW Catch weight by species
FL Survey-specific flex file (additional fields)
LT Marine litter assessment

From April 2012, all data products include a DateofCalculation field for tracking changes.

How do I acknowledge use of DATRAS data?

Follow the ICES Data Policy. Example citations:

ICES Database of Trawl Surveys (DATRAS). ICES, Copenhagen. https://datras.ices.dk

ICES DATRAS, Extraction [DATE]. [Survey name] data [year range]. ICES, Copenhagen. https://datras.ices.dk

What are the data use restrictions?

DATRAS data are available under the ICES open access data policy (adopted 2006, updated 2012). Aggregated data and raw data are freely available under these conditions.

For Data Users

Downloading data

How do I access trawl survey data?

Survey data and derived products can be downloaded from the DATRAS download page. Note that the web portal limits downloads to 1 million records. For larger datasets, split the download into smaller parts (e.g. by year or quarter) or use the ICES web services / {obus} parquet access (dr_con()) for efficient bulk access without record limits.

Why are not all data products available for all surveys?

DATRAS data products are developed and verified in cooperation with the relevant ICES Expert Groups. This is a time-consuming process, and newer surveys may not yet have derived products. Currently CPUEL is available for 8 of the 29 surveys (BITS, EVHOE, IE-IGFS, NS-IBTS, ROCKALL, SCOROC, SCOWCGFS, SWC-IBTS).

What are “Standard species”?

Standard species are the target species of a given survey. While all surveys aim to record all species caught, each survey has one or more target species for which detailed biological data (sex, maturity, age, individual weight) are collected. Age-based data products (CPUEA, IDX) are therefore available only for standard species. Refer to the relevant survey manual for the standard species list.

Understanding the data format

How do I understand the DATRAS exchange file format?

Exchange data are the raw survey data as submitted by national institutes. Each file contains three record types:

Record Content
HH Haul Header — one row per haul; gear, position, timing, validity
HL Haul-level Length — catch by length class per species per haul
CA Catch-at-age — individual biological measurements

The three record types share a common set of haul identification fields and are linked by the composite haul key .id (constructed from Survey, Year, Quarter, Country, Platform (Ship), Gear, StationName (StNo), HaulNumber (HaulNo)). In the raw CSV submission format, these correspond to the first 11 fields common to all record types.

What does “−9” stand for?

−9 is the DATRAS sentinel value for a missing or inapplicable field. It is not a real measurement. In the IMBUS standardised product ({obus}, parquet files) all −9 values are replaced with proper NA.

What are haul duration, vessel speed, and trawl parameters?

Standard values for HaulDuration (HaulDur), vessel speed, and gear parameters are defined in the survey manuals. Actual values as recorded during the haul are reported in the HH record fields HaulDuration (HaulDur), GroundSpeed, Distance, DoorSpread, WingSpread, and WarpLength.

Are catch figures adjusted for hauling time?

This depends on DataType in the HH record. See DataType and HL field interpretation for details.

Are all species reported in DATRAS?

Surveys intend to record all species caught, but it is the responsibility of the national data submitter whether all species are reported. The fields SpeciesValidity (SpecVal) in HL (field 26 in the raw HH record) and SpeciesCategory (CatIdentifier) (field 27) provide further context. Some surveys report a limited species list; refer to the survey manual.

In CA records, mainly standard survey species are reported.

What is SpeciesValidity (SpecVal)?

SpeciesValidity in HL records specifies how the species was handled:

Value Meaning
1 Full record: lengths, numbers, weights — use in standard products
0 Invalid
4 Total number only; no lengths measured
5 Presence/absence only
6 Weight only
7 Numbers and weights; no lengths
10 Lengths and numbers; no weights

Only SpeciesValidity == "1" records are used in DATRAS-derived data products. Records with other values may still be of value for biodiversity or non-standard analyses.

SpeciesValidity is also linked to haul validity: if the haul was invalid or used for calibration, SpeciesValidity will reflect this.

What species reference system is used?

Species codes currently used for submissions are WoRMS AphiaIDs (SpeciesCodeType (SpecCodeType) = "W"). In historical submissions ITIS TSN ("T") and NODC ("N") codes were used. For cross-era analysis, all submitted codes are mapped to validated WoRMS AphiaIDs in the field ValidAphiaID (Valid_Aphia / Valid_WoRMS) in HL and CA records. Always use ValidAphiaID as the join key for species — never the raw SpeciesCode (SpecCode).

In derived data products (CPUEL, CPUEA, IDX), species are identified by valid Latin names or AphiaIDs.

Where can I find species names for codes in the file?

Species names can be matched to codes via the DATRAS Species Query Tool or directly via the WoRMS database.

If work in R you can also try this:

icesVocab::findAphia(c("cod", "haddock", "saithe"))
icesVocab::findAphia("Gadus morhuha", latin = TRUE)

Where can I find information about species maximum length?

Per-survey maximum length classes (mm) are listed on the DATRAS reporting format page (DATSU). Note that this list is static and was last updated in January 2011.

What area codes are used in DATRAS?

Area information appears in several places:

  • Haul positions: ShootLatitude/ShootLongitude and HaulLatitude/HaulLongitude in HH, reported as decimal degrees.
  • Statistical rectangle: StatisticalRectangle (StatRec) in HH, based on shooting position (unless the majority of the haul was in the adjacent rectangle).
  • Depth strata: DepthStratum (Stratum) in HH, for surveys with depth-stratified sampling.
  • Age-sampling areas: AreaCode and AreaType in CA, indicating whether the age sample is haul-level (AreaType == "H") or area-pooled. Always check AreaType before joining CA records to HH.

ICES statistical rectangles are described at the ICES maps page.

Do DATRAS data pass any quality validation?

Yes. Before upload, all files pass the DATSU screening utility, which applies logical, value, and vocabulary checks. Critical errors must be corrected before the file can be uploaded; warnings are non-critical. Weight-length outlier graphs are also provided as an additional screening tool.

Every year, responsible expert groups review their data and report irrelevancies to the DATRAS team. If you find erroneous data, contact DATRAS administration (DATRASAdministration@ices.dk).

CPUE and derived products

What is CPUE in DATRAS?

CPUE (catch per unit effort) in DATRAS is the catch in numbers of individuals of the same species, adjusted to one hour of hauling.

If I compare CPUE per length values to the exchange file, they don’t match. Why?

Exchange files contain data as submitted, potentially in DataType R (raw), S (sub-sampled), or C (catch per unit effort). CPUE data products display numbers raised to one hour of hauling. To compare, you must apply the appropriate raising formula to the exchange data. See DataType and HL field interpretation for the correct formula per DataType.

What do zeroes mean in a CPUE product?

A CPUE value of zero means the species was absent from that haul but was caught by at least one vessel in the same survey/year/quarter. Zero records are automatically added to all hauls where the species was absent. Explicit zero rows are required for unbiased abundance indices.

A species may also have a zero if the data submitter observed but did not record biological data. Refer to the exchange file for details.

A species is missing from CPUE for some years/quarters. Why?

Records for a species in a CPUE product appear only if at least one submitter observed and reported it in that year/quarter. If no submitter reported the species in a given year/quarter, no records (including zeroes) will be present in the product.

Why are age records limited to age_6 or age_10 in age-based products?

Age-based products are designed according to requirements from the responsible ICES Expert Group. Groups specify a plus group age, above which all fish are aggregated (e.g. NS-IBTS uses plus group age 6: all fish aged 6+ are recorded as age 6).

What is country code DUM (DUMMY)?

Country code DUM was used for reporting historical age data from Danish commercial vessels with platform codes DCA and HOL in 1983–1995.

Where are BITS data before 1991?

BITS started in the early 1980s but was not standardised until 1991. The responsible expert group (WGBIFS) decided that only data from 1991 onward should be available via DATRAS. Contact WGBIFS for access to earlier data.

Where do I find survey abundance indices?

DATRAS provides CPUE-by-length and CPUE-by-age products for standard species. These are accessible via dr_get("IDX", ...) in {obus}. Note that no weight-based products are currently available from DATRAS.

Is there a tool to convert age to weight?

Not directly. You can use the CA records (including individual weight measurements where available) to construct your own age–weight relationship.

Can I see when data were last corrected?

The DATRAS Submission Status page shows when the last cruise submission was made. The current DATRAS version does not show what was changed in a re-submission.

For Data Submitters

General

What is the deadline for my submission?

Submission deadlines are listed on the DATRAS home page. Contact DATRAS administration if you are unsure.

How should my data file be formatted?

DATRAS accepts CSV files (comma-separated, decimal point ., no blank fields — use −9 for missing values, no headers). One file should contain all record types (HH, HL, CA) for the same year, quarter, country, vessel, and gear. All fields from the reporting format must be present.

Which units and codes should be used?

Field descriptions, units, and valid codes are in the DATRAS Reporting Format documentation and in the ICES Vocabulary Server (https://vocab.ices.dk/). Within {obus}, valid codes are also available via dr_lookup_vocabulary.

How do I get access to the DATRAS upload page?

Write to DATRAS administration and the Chair of the associated ICES Expert Group with your submitter details and access request.

Submission process

How do I submit my file?

Direct upload is available for most surveys from the DATRAS uploading page. Follow the instructions in “How to upload data into DATRAS” (available in the Documents section).

What quality control does my file pass?

Files pass the DATSU screening utility before upload:

  • Critical errors must be corrected; the file cannot be uploaded until they are resolved.
  • Warnings are non-critical; the submitter decides whether to correct them.
  • Information messages do not affect uploading.

Screening results are displayed on the DATRAS page and sent to the submitter’s email. If a screening message is unclear, click the question mark in the screening results table for more detail. If still unclear, contact DATRAS administration with your file and a screenshot.

What is error grouping in DATSU?

When the same error occurs many times, DATSU groups them into a single entry with a count. The submitter can expand the group to see individual occurrences. This avoids scrolling through thousands of identical messages.

I get “the survey selected does not include the country present in the file”. What is wrong?

Check:

  1. The country code matches the vocabulary at https://vocab.ices.dk/?ref=4.
  2. Numbers use a decimal point (.), not a decimal comma.
  3. Your account has upload rights for this survey. Contact DATRAS administration if not.

I cannot find my uploaded data on the download page.

Check the DATRAS Submission Status page. If your submission is listed there with the correct upload time, the upload was successful. Updates to the download page may take a few days due to manual quality-control processing. If the data are needed urgently, contact DATRAS administration.

Can I make a partial re-submission by haul or record type?

No. DATRAS requires a complete re-submission per year, quarter, country, vessel, and gear.

What happens to existing data when I re-submit?

Existing data are overwritten when the data warehouse is updated. Always keep a copy of previous submissions. Note: if you re-submit with different key values (year, quarter, country, vessel, gear), the old data will not be overwritten — contact DATRAS administration to remove duplicates.

Can I document what was changed in a re-submission?

Yes — use the remarks box at the final upload step. You are also welcome to email DATRAS administration describing the corrections made.

Technical issues for data submitters

DataType and HL field interpretation

DataType is set at the haul level (HH record) and governs how HL fields must be interpreted. It is the most common source of silent errors when working with exchange data.

DataType Description CPUE formula
R Raw; catch sorted, some species may be sub-sampled. SubsamplingFactor ≥ 1 n_haul = Σ(NumberAtLength × SubsamplingFactor)
S Bulk unsorted catch sub-sampled before sorting. SubsamplingFactor > 1 Same as R
P Size-stratified subsampling — different SubsamplingFactor per size grade. Do not sum TotalNumber across strata. Same row-level formula as R, applied per stratum
C Catch pre-raised to 1 hour of hauling. SubsamplingFactor = 1 n_hour = NumberAtLength; n_haul = NumberAtLength × HaulDuration / 60

The {obus} function dr_HL_standardised() applies the correct formula for all DataTypes automatically. For full field-by-field specifications and empirical notes on DataType misuse in submissions, see DATRAS Technical Reference §DataType.

What is SpeciesCategory (CatIdentifier), and how do I use it?

SpeciesCategory (CatIdentifier) is the ID assigned to a size- or weight-based subsampling stratum for a given species and sex within a haul. It is used when a truly representative sub-sample cannot be selected from the whole catch — for example, when larger fish are sorted away and measured separately with a different sub-sampling factor.

  • Assign SpeciesCategory = 1 to the first stratum and SpeciesCategory = 2, 3, … to additional strata (up to 5 per species per haul).
  • If no size categorisation was applied, use SpeciesCategory = 1 throughout.
  • Reporting different sex categories for the same species does not require different SpeciesCategory values — keep SpeciesCategory = 1 and vary SpeciesSex (Sex) instead.

See the NS-IBTS survey manual (section “Length composition”) for worked examples.

How is Day/Night defined?

Daytime is defined as the period from 15 minutes before sunrise to 15 minutes after sunset, following NOAA’s solar calculations. The derived field DayNight in HH is populated by the ICES datacenter from the shooting position and time.

Which area/position should I report?

  • Coordinates: ShootLatitude/ShootLongitude and HaulLatitude/HaulLongitude in HH, as decimal degrees.
  • Statistical rectangle: StatisticalRectangle (StatRec) in HH, based on shooting position.
  • Depth strata: DepthStratum (Stratum) in HH, if sampling is depth-stratified.
  • CA area codes: AreaCode and AreaType in CA, specifying haul-level vs. area-pooled age sampling.

How do I submit area-based CA data without corresponding HL data?

CA records must always be related to a parent HL record. If age sampling was area-based, create a dummy HL record by duplicating fields 2–12 from the CA record and setting all other HL fields to −9.

Example CA record:

CA,1,SCO,SCO3,GOV,60,-9,P,S01,1,2010,W,126417,2,3,0,140,U,-9,-9,3,1,-9

Corresponding dummy HL record:

HL,1,SCO,SCO3,GOV,60,-9,P,S01,1,2010,W,126417,0,-9,-9,-9,-9,-9,-9,-9,-9,-9,-9

Species codes and SpeciesCodeType (SpecCodeType)

Current submissions use WoRMS AphiaIDs (SpeciesCodeType = "W"). Historical data used ITIS TSN ("T") and NODC ("N") codes. All codes are mapped to current WoRMS AphiaIDs in the field ValidAphiaID (Valid_Aphia / Valid_WoRMS). For all analysis, use ValidAphiaID as the species join key.

Annex I: DataType examples in exchange data

HH record: DataType values

DataType Description
S Sub-sampled: catch sub-sampled before sorting; all SubsamplingFactor (SubFactor) values > 1
R Recorded by haul: catch sorted; some species may be sub-sampled; SubsamplingFactor ≥ 1
P Size-stratified (pseudocategory): different SubsamplingFactor per size stratum within a species; uses SpeciesCategory (CatIdentifier) to label strata
C CPUE: data pre-raised to one hour of hauling; all SubsamplingFactor = 1

HL record: field definitions

Field (new name) Old name Description
SubsamplingFactor SubFactor Sub-sampling factor by haul, species, sex, and SpeciesCategory
NumberAtLength HLNoAtLngt Number of fish measured in the (sub-)sample at this length class
SubsampledNumber NoMeas Sum of NumberAtLength across length classes, by haul × species × sex × SpeciesCategory
TotalNumber TotalNo SubsamplingFactor × SubsampledNumber (DataType R/S/P); raised count per hour (DataType C)

Example 1: DataType S — 1,140 fish, 3 species

Catch: 1,140 fish   SubsamplingFactor = 2
→ Measure 570 fish (sub-sample)

  400 Plaice          150 Whiting         20 Cod
  SubFactor = 2       SubFactor = 8       SubFactor = 1
  NoMeas = 200        NoMeas = 75/2 = ...  NoMeas = 20
  Measure 200         Measure sub-sample   Measure all 20

For Plaice:
TotalNumber = 400, SubsamplingFactor = 2, SubsampledNumber = 200
Verify: 400 = 200 × 2 ✓
NumberAtLength values sum to 200 (= SubsampledNumber) ✓

Example 2: DataType R — size-stratified (DataType P encoding)

Species with two size grades in one haul:

SpeciesCategory (CatIdentifier) Size range SubsamplingFactor (SubFactor) Fish measured TotalNumber (TotalNo)
1 Small (100–160 mm) 5.9 61 ≈ 360
2 Large (210–420 mm) 1.0 168 168

Correct haul total: 360 + 168 = 528 fish
Formula: n_haul = Σ(NumberAtLength × SubsamplingFactor) applied row by row — correct for all DataTypes including size-stratified R and P hauls.
Do not sum TotalNumber across SpeciesCategory strata — it is the stratum total.

IMBUS Additions

The questions below were not in the original ICES FAQ. They arose from exploratory analysis of the DATRAS exchange tables conducted as part of IMBUS WP2.

What do HL records with a missing LengthClass represent?

Note

[IMBUS] This question was not in the original ICES FAQ. It arose from IMBUS WP2 exploratory analysis of the DATRAS HL table.

In the HL exchange table, records where LengthClass is NA (encoded as −9 in raw CSV files) are catch-totals rows. They record aggregate catch information for a species within a haul but carry no individual length measurement. Every such row also has NumberAtLength = NA. They are distinct from length-frequency rows in both purpose and content.

Catch-totals rows typically carry one or more of:

Field Content
TotalNumber Total count of fish in the haul (raised; or per hour for DataType C)
SubsampledNumber Number of fish actually handled or weighed
SpeciesCategoryWeight Catch weight for the species/category
SubsamplingFactor Raising factor (may be 1 if all fish were counted)

These rows arise in two situations:

  1. Species measured by count or weight only — where SpeciesValidity ≠ 1 (e.g. 4 = total number only, 6 = weight only, 7 = numbers and weights but no lengths). In this case the catch-totals row is the only HL record for that species.
  2. Companion summary rows — present alongside a full length-frequency series for the same species/haul (typically SpeciesValidity = 1). The catch-totals row then summarises what the length rows describe individually.

Key implication for analysis: always filter !is.na(LengthClass) (and !is.na(NumberAtLength)) before computing CPUE or length distributions. Catch-totals rows cannot be raised via sum(NumberAtLength × SubsamplingFactor) — they carry no NumberAtLength. The {obus} function dr_add_n_and_cpue() handles this automatically.

TotalNumber in catch-totals rows is useful as a cross-check against the raised sum from the length-frequency rows, but note that its scope (all-sex total vs. sex-specific) is not consistent across surveys; see the DATRAS data issues for a survey-by-survey reconciliation.

What is the difference between DataType “P” and “R”?

Short answer: DataType R (raw catch) is for hauls where the entire catch is sorted and some species are sub-sampled at a single rate. DataType P (pseudocategory) is for hauls where the catch is sorted into multiple size grades, each with its own sub-sampling rate.

When you would encounter each:

DataType Scenario Sub-sampling approach
R Regular haul One species, one sub-sample rate per species
P Size-stratified One species, multiple size grades, each grade sub-sampled separately
S Bulk sub-sample Entire catch sub-sampled before sorting
C Pre-raised Lab already converted to CPUE (per hour)

Example: same species, two size grades in one haul

DataType SpeciesCategory Size range SubsamplingFactor Measured TotalNumber
P 1 Small (100–160 mm) 5.9 61 ≈ 360
2 Large (210–420 mm) 1.0 168 168

For both P and R, calculate haul total identically: n_haul = Σ(NumberAtLength × SubsamplingFactor) per row = (61 × 5.9) + (168 × 1.0) = 528 fish.

Why the distinction exists:

  • DataType P explicitly signals that size-stratification is present, so downstream users understand that multiple SpeciesCategory rows per species per haul are expected, and each TotalNumber corresponds to a size stratum.
  • DataType R signals that there is one sub-sampling rate per species; users can be confident that summing by species will yield the haul total.

Recommended practice for users: - Always use the row-level formula: n_haul = Σ(NumberAtLength × SubsamplingFactor) per haul and species - The {obus} function dr_add_n_and_cpue() handles all DataTypes correctly

Why do we have more than one value for SpeciesValidity within the same tow (.id) for the same species?

SpeciesValidity is a record type field — it describes what kind of data is present for a species within a haul, not whether the haul is valid. The HL exchange format allows more than one record type for the same species and haul, and some surveys use this deliberately.

A typical scenario: a species was fully length-measured ("1", full record), but the crew also recorded a total count for a size group that was not individually measured ("4", total number only), or noted a presence/absence for bycatch ("5"). Both rows share the same .id × aphia group, so SpeciesValidity is not constant within that group.

This is intentional, not an error. BTS and Can-Mar are the main surveys that submit multiple record types for the same species and haul. Across all surveys, ~2,786 of ~2.6 million HL groups (0.1%) show this pattern. The most common co-occurrences are {1, 5} and {4, 7}.

Worked example. The following rows are taken directly from haul BTS:2024:3:BE:1186:BT4S:40:1 for Sepia officinalis (aphia 141444, common cuttlefish). Only the DevelopmentStage = NA rows are shown (the egg rows, DevelopmentStage = "E", follow the same pattern):

LengthClass NumberAtLength TotalNumber SpeciesValidity
10 1 18 1
15 1 18 1
20 2 18 1
25 6 18 1
… … 18 1
110 1 18 1
NA NA 18 5

The ten SpeciesValidity = "1" rows carry the full length-frequency distribution (LengthClass 10–110 mm, NumberAtLength filled). The final row, SpeciesValidity = "5", is a supplementary presence/absence marker: it carries no length or count data of its own (LengthClass = NA, NumberAtLength = NA) but coexists in the same .id × aphia group. This is the intended semantics of SpeciesValidity = "5" as defined in the DATRAS format.

Can-Mar exception. Can-Mar uses SpeciesValidity = "5" differently — on rows that carry actual LengthClass and NumberAtLength values. Across 535 Can-Mar groups with both "1" and "5" rows containing length data, 42% have entirely distinct length classes between the two record types (the "5" rows add lengths not in the "1" rows), 20% are full duplicates, and 38% partially overlap. The reason for this split is not documented in the DATRAS submission format. Filtering to SpeciesValidity == "1" in Can-Mar will therefore discard real length data in at least 42% of these groups. See Can-Mar SpeciesValidity=“5” anomaly in the issues document.

Practical implication: if you aggregate over .id × aphia without also grouping on SpeciesValidity, rows from different record types will be silently mixed together. For most surveys (including BTS), filter to SpeciesValidity == "1" before aggregating to retain only the full length-frequency records. For Can-Mar, the appropriate treatment of SpeciesValidity = "5" rows with length data is currently unresolved.

See the SpeciesValidity vocabulary in D2.2 for the full code list, and HL grouping key constancy violations in the issues document for per-survey statistics.
